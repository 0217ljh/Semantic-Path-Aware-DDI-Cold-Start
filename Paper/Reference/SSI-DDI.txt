Title: SSI-DDI: substructure-substructure interactions for drug-drug interaction prediction
Authors: Arnold K. Nyamabo, Hui Yu, Jian-Yu Shi
Venue+Year: Briefings in Bioinformatics, 22(6), 2021 (Problem Solving Protocol)
arXiv-or-DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/bib/bbab133
PDF filename: SSI-DDI.pdf

## TL;DR
A molecular-structure GNN that decomposes DDI prediction into pairwise interactions between
learned chemical substructures of the two drugs. Each of L GAT layers produces one drug-level
substructure vector (READOUT via SAGPooling); a co-attention layer weights every substructure
pair, and the DDI score is a relation-typed bilinear sum over substructure pairs. Operates on
raw molecular graphs (SMILES→RDKit graph), no KG. Demonstrated in both transductive and
inductive (cold-start) settings. (Abstract; Method; Figure 1)

## Problem & Contributions
- Problem: predict whether a drug pair (Gx, Gy) has interaction of type Ir, framed as
  f: G x G x I -> [0,1] (Method, "Problem settings").
- Motivation claim: DDIs are fundamentally caused by chemical substructure interactions, not
  whole-drug structures; prior methods rely on similarity assumption or handcrafted fingerprints
  (Abstract; Introduction, pages 1-2).
- Contributions (Introduction, page 2): (1) a model that explicitly treats substructures as
  independent entities whose pairwise interactions determine DDI; (2) learns substructures
  directly from molecular graphs and weights each substructure-pair interaction with a
  co-attention mechanism (interpretable by-product). Claims it works in inductive/cold-start
  setting on drugs not seen in training.

## Method / Core mechanism (equations in words + notation)
- Input: each drug is a molecular graph G=(V,E); atom node feature hi in R^55 (atomic symbol
  one-hot 44, degree, implicit valence, formal charge, radical electrons, hybridization one-hot
  5, aromaticity, #H). Each interaction type Ir is a learnable matrix Mr in R^{D x D}.
  (Inputs; Experiments/Dataset, pages 3, 5).
- Graph convolution: L GAT layers (Eqs 1-5). Node update = linear transform (Eq 1) + attention
  weights alpha_ij over neighbors via LeakyReLU on concatenated features (Eqs 2-3) + weighted
  aggregation (Eq 4) + ELU activation (Eq 5). Multi-head attention with K heads, concatenated
  (Eq 6). LayerNorm added before activation (Eq 7).
- Chemical substructure extraction: after l GAT layers, node i's vector encodes a substructure
  centered at i spanning its 1..l-hop neighbors (Figure 2). At each layer a READOUT aggregates
  node vectors z_i^(l) weighted by learnable coefficient beta_i to give drug-level substructure
  vector g_x^(l) (Eq 8). beta computed by SAGPooling: beta = softmax(GNN(A,X)) (Eq 9). So each
  GAT layer yields exactly one substructure representation per drug.
- Co-attention relevance: gamma_ij = b^T tanh(Wx g_x^(i) + Wy g_y^(j)) for i,j = 1..L (Eq 10),
  the relevance of the interaction between drug-x substructure from layer i and drug-y
  substructure from layer j. Distinct Wx, Wy; no activation (to allow negative scores for
  non-interacting pairs).
- Prediction: P(Gx,Gy,r) = sigmoid( sum_i sum_j gamma_ij * g_x^(i)^T Mr g_y^(j) ) (Eq 11).
  Interaction-typed bilinear form over substructure pairs, each weighted by co-attention.
- Loss: binary cross-entropy (Eq 12); negatives generated by corrupting one drug of each
  positive triple (strategy from ref [47]).
- Setup: L=4 GAT layers, K=2 heads, 32 features/head (64 output), Mr in R^{64x64}, batch 1024,
  Adam, weight decay 5e-4, lr = 1e-2 * 0.96^t (Setup, page 5).

## Key results & numbers (with Table/Fig/Section pointers)
- Dataset: DrugBank v5.0.3 as preprocessed/split (6:2:2) by Ryu et al. [4]: 1704 drugs, 191,400
  DDIs, 86 interaction types (Experiments/Dataset, page 5).
- Transductive (Table 1, page 6): SSI-DDI ACC 0.9447, AUROC 0.9838, AUPRC 0.9814. Beats
  MHCADDI, GoGNN on AUPRC; comparable/slightly lower AUROC vs MR-GNN (0.9976) and DeepDDI
  (0.9976), but authors argue AUROC is unreliable under class imbalance for multi-class models.
  SSI-DDI best on ACC and AUPRC (AUPRC margin ~4.04% over next).
- Inductive / cold-start (Table 2, page 6), mean +/- SD over 3 runs, split by drugs:
  DS1 (both drugs new): SSI-DDI ACC 65.02, AUROC 72.38, AUPRC 72.83.
  DS2 (one new, one old): SSI-DDI ACC 73.35, AUROC 80.92, AUPRC 80.80.
  Competitive with MHCADDI; GoGNN excluded (needs DDI topology, unavailable for new drugs).
  Notable performance drop from transductive to inductive noted (page 6-7).
- Ablation (Figure 4, page 7): co-attention is important; a 3-layer model WITH co-attention beats
  a 5-layer model without it.
- Case study (Tables/Figs 3-5, pages 7-8): PDE5 inhibitors x organic nitrates; nitrate functional
  group shown to drive DDI prediction.

## Relevance to our cold-start low-rank DDI paper (§2.1 taxonomy slot; molecular vs KG; warm vs cold; how we'd cite)
- §2.1 taxonomy slot: MOLECULAR-substructure-based (substructure-substructure interaction) GNN
  method. Uses raw molecular graphs only; NO knowledge graph.
- Warm vs cold: warm-start-capable AND explicitly cold-start-capable. This is one of the earliest
  molecular DDI methods to define and evaluate the inductive/cold-start split (DS1 both-new,
  DS2 one-new) that our S1/S2 setting mirrors. Their DS1 = both drugs unseen matches our
  cold-start definition.
- How we'd cite: (a) as a canonical molecular-substructure baseline in §2.1; (b) as an early
  source of the DS1/DS2 cold-start drug-split protocol; (c) to note that molecular-only methods
  transfer to unseen drugs via shared substructures but suffer a notable transductive->inductive
  drop (their own Table 2 vs Table 1), motivating KG/relational signal for cold-start.

## Do-NOT-overclaim / caveats
- DrugBank here has 1704 drugs / 191,400 DDIs / 86 types (page 5). Note other papers (e.g.
  HDN-DDI) report 1706 drugs / 191,808 DDIs for "the same" DrugBank; do not conflate exact counts.
- The paper's DDI task is MULTI-TYPE triple scoring (86 types), evaluated per-triple with
  generated negatives; it is not the standard multi-class-only formulation of Ryu/Deng datasets.
- AUROC for SSI-DDI is slightly BELOW MR-GNN/DeepDDI in transductive Table 1; authors argue AUROC
  is unreliable under imbalance. Do not state SSI-DDI beats all baselines on all metrics.
- Cold-start numbers are only "competitive with MHCADDI," not dominant; authors themselves report
  a significant performance drop in the inductive setting.
- Limitation stated by authors: performance is sensitive to drug ordering; possible noise leaked
  during substructure extraction (Conclusion, page 8).
- Cold-start experiments use ratio k=1/5 new drugs and lr=1e-3*0.96^t (differs from transductive).
