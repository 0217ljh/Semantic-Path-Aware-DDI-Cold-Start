Title: Fine-tuning Happens in Tiny Subspaces: Exploring Intrinsic Task-specific Subspaces of Pre-trained Language Models
Authors: Zhong Zhang, Bang Liu, Junming Shao (UESTC; Shenzhen Institute for Advanced Study; Mila & Universite de Montreal)
Venue+Year: ACL 2023 (main; per arXiv metadata / GLUE+BERT/RoBERTa study). Listed on arXiv as 2023.
arXiv-or-DOI: arXiv:2305.17446v2 [cs.CL], 1 Aug 2023
PDF filename: Fine-tuning Happens in Tiny Subspaces.pdf

## TL;DR
Rather than measuring intrinsic dimension in a RANDOM subspace, this paper recovers an explicit, task-specific low-dimensional subspace by running SVD on the fine-tuning trajectory (the sequence of parameter checkpoints) and keeping the top principal directions. Re-parameterizing a PLM's encoder into this per-layer subspace and training only ~32 free parameters per layer (64 for MNLI) recovers nearly full fine-tuning performance on GLUE with BERT and RoBERTa -- far better than a random subspace of the same size. The exact claim: fine-tuning of PLMs happens in tiny task-specific subspaces of order tens of dimensions per layer. (Abstract; Sec. 1; Sec. 3-4; Table 1.)

## Problem & Contributions
Problem. Why can a 100M+ parameter PLM be fine-tuned effectively with few labeled samples, and must all parameters be tuned? Prior work (Li et al. 2018; Aghajanyan et al. 2021) showed low intrinsic dimension via RANDOM projection, but a random subspace injects task-irrelevant directions and is not the actual subspace fine-tuning uses. (Sec. 1-2.)
Contributions. (1) Interpret adaptability as fine-tuning happening in tiny INTRINSIC TASK-SPECIFIC subspaces, and propose to uncover them via principal directions of the fine-tuning trajectory (SVD) rather than random projection (Sec. 1, Sec. 3.2). (2) On GLUE with BERT-base and RoBERTa-base, show ~32 free params/layer in the intrinsic subspace approach full fine-tuning, and beat random subspaces substantially (Sec. 4.2, Table 1). (3) Show a certain transferability of these subspaces across tasks (inductive setting, Fig. 2) and a unified 8-dim task subspace (Sec. 4.4, Table 2). (4) Identify "outlier dimensions" (specific weight parameters, not output channels) that emerge only in the intrinsic subspace and are crucial to task knowledge (Sec. 4.5, Tables 3-4).

## Method / Core mechanism (equations in words + notation)
Notation: theta^D in R^D is the flattened parameter vector (per encoder layer, e.g. 7,087,872 dims for BERT-base); theta_0^D the frozen init; V in R^{D x t} the learned projection (task-specific); theta^t in R^t the trainable low-dim vector.
Baseline random re-parameterization (Eq. 1, in words): full params = frozen init + random projection P applied to a low-dim vector, theta^D = theta_0^D + P*theta^d, with theta_0 and P frozen (the Li et al. 2018 / Aghajanyan et al. 2021 setup).
Discovering the intrinsic subspace (Eq. 2, in words): collect the fine-tuning trajectory of checkpoints {theta_i^D} into a matrix W (t rows = checkpoints, D columns = params), then take a (compact) SVD W = U * Sigma * V^T. The right singular vectors (columns of V) are the principal directions of the trajectory and form an orthonormal basis of the low-dim subspace the trajectory lies in. A (t-1)-dim subspace needs only t trajectory points to determine.
Fine-tuning in the intrinsic subspace (Eq. 3, in words): replace random P with the learned V, so full params = frozen init + V applied to the trainable low-dim vector, theta^D = theta_0^D + V*theta^t; freeze theta_0^D and V, train only theta^t. Applied layer-wise (12 projection matrices for BERT), with embedding and final classifier left in full space.
Variance-reduction ensemble (Eq. 4, in words): because a single theta^t is sensitive to initialization, average h=16 independently-initialized low-dim vectors; this reduces variance without changing the subspace dimensionality / degrees of freedom.
Settings: trajectory length (subspace dim) t = 32 for all tasks except MNLI = 64; learning rate for theta^t = 0.01; results averaged over 5 seeds (Sec. 4.1, Appendix A.1).

## Key results & numbers (with Table/Fig/Section pointers)
- Transductive (subspace from the same task), Table 1, GLUE Avg. of 8 tasks, dim=32 (MNLI 64):
  BERT-Full 82.13 vs BERT-Intrinsic 81.21 vs BERT-Random 72.54 vs BERT-Freeze 69.81.
  RoBERTa-Full 85.43 vs RoBERTa-Intrinsic 84.40 vs RoBERTa-Random 71.13 vs RoBERTa-Freeze 57.04.
  => Intrinsic subspace nearly matches full fine-tuning and is ~9-13 avg points above the random subspace of equal size.
- Ablation on subspace dimension (Tables 5-6, Appendix A.2): performance rises with dim; e.g. BERT MRPC 75.05 (dim=8) -> 77.94 (16) -> 84.31 (32); RoBERTa MRPC 75.59 -> 78.20 -> 87.21. Small dims already give strong results on easy tasks.
- Inductive / transfer (Fig. 2): subspaces transferred FROM other tasks still beat the random baseline; performance drop (vs same-task subspace) is small for many pairs. Small-data source tasks (CoLA, MRPC, STS-B, RTE) transfer better than large-data ones (SST-2, QQP, MNLI, QNLI), since complex tasks need higher-dim subspaces.
- Unified task subspace (Sec. 4.4, Table 2): an 8-dim subspace built by SVD over checkpoints pooled across tasks lets all GLUE tasks be fine-tuned near full performance (BERT-Unified Avg. 82.48 vs BERT-Full 82.13; RoBERTa-Unified 85.83 vs 85.43). A zero-shot 7-dim variant (target task's checkpoint excluded) drops but still beats random. Cosine similarities between per-task theta^t vectors are near zero (Fig. 3) -> the unified subspace holds disentangled, task-distributed knowledge.
- Outlier dimensions (Sec. 4.5, Tables 3-4): sparse individual weights (defined at parameter granularity, ~7M dims/layer) show abnormal spikes ONLY in the intrinsic subspace, not random. Disabling ~0.3% of encoder params (those >3 sigma) collapses performance (e.g. BERT-Outlier CoLA 0.00, STS-B 0.00 vs BERT-Full 59.37 / 89.08), whereas disabling the same number of random weights barely hurts (Table 3). Disabling only the single top outlier per layer has negligible effect.

## Relevance to our cold-start low-rank DDI paper (RW bucket; how we'd cite; concrete DELTA vs our method)
RW bucket: Stream B (intrinsic dimension / task subspaces). This is the strongest "how low-dimensional is the adaptation update" evidence in our library, and it sharpens the Li-et-al. premise from RANDOM low dimension to LEARNED, task-specific low dimension. It directly supports our design choice that the adaptation / per-pair residual should live in a small learned subspace (small rank a), and that a structured task-specific basis outperforms a generic one.
How we would cite: "Beyond random-subspace evidence (Li et al. 2018), Zhang et al. (2023) show the actual fine-tuning update lies in a tiny task-specific subspace (order tens of dimensions per layer) recovered from the training trajectory, and that this learned subspace substantially outperforms a random subspace of equal size. This motivates our choice to learn a small, typed low-rank basis (M_A) rather than adopt a generic or full-rank residual." Key quotable claim: fine-tuning happens in tiny subspaces (~32 free params/layer recover near-full performance on GLUE), Table 1.
Concrete DELTA vs our method: (1) Their subspace is recovered POST-HOC by SVD on a fine-tuning trajectory of a SEEN task; our low-rank residual H = H_base + M_A * M_B is a FORWARD, architecturally-imposed structure trained end-to-end -- we never require a trajectory to be recorded and decomposed. (2) Their basis V is per-layer weight-space directions of a language model; our basis M_A is a TYPED biomedical mediator basis over a KG, and M_B are per-pair coefficients -- semantics differ entirely. (3) They study warm fine-tuning of a task whose data is available; our thesis is about the TRANSFERABLE-TO-UNSEEN signal in cold-start DDI (both drugs unseen), which their setting never tests. Their "transferability" (Fig. 2) is task-to-task subspace reuse, not entity-level cold-start generalization. (4) Their outlier-dimension finding is a diagnostic about which individual weights matter, orthogonal to our cold-safety concern; we should NOT import the outlier claim as support for our method.

## Safe-to-cite claims (section pointers)
- Fine-tuning trajectory lies in a low-dim subspace recoverable by SVD of stacked checkpoints; right singular vectors are the principal directions (Eq. 2, Sec. 3.2).
- Re-parameterization theta^D = theta_0^D + V*theta^t, freeze theta_0 and V, train only theta^t (Eq. 3, Sec. 3.3).
- ~32 free params/layer (64 for MNLI) in the intrinsic subspace recover near-full GLUE performance and beat random subspaces (Table 1, Sec. 4.2).
- Intrinsic subspaces show a certain cross-task transferability (Fig. 2, Sec. 4.3) and a unified 8-dim task subspace exists (Table 2, Sec. 4.4).
- Performance increases with subspace dimension (Tables 5-6, Appendix A.2).
- Builds explicitly on Li et al. (2018) intrinsic dimension and Aghajanyan et al. (2021) PLM intrinsic dim (hundreds-to-thousands); reports BERT-base MRPC intrinsic dim of 1861 from Aghajanyan et al. (Sec. 3.1).

## Do-NOT-overclaim / caveats
- Layer-wise setting only: subspaces are LOCAL (per encoder layer), not a single global subspace; the authors state the existence of a global task subspace is unascertained (Limitations). Do not claim a single global low-rank subspace for the whole model.
- Evaluated only on natural language UNDERSTANDING (GLUE), only encoder-only BERT/RoBERTa-base; no generation, no decoder-only/encoder-decoder, no large models (Limitations). Do not extrapolate to other architectures/scales or to DDI/graph models.
- The subspace is recovered from a completed fine-tuning trajectory of the SAME (seen) task -- this is not a cold-start / unseen-entity result. Do not cite it as evidence for cold-start transfer.
- Transferability (Fig. 2) is modest and correlates with task scale/similarity; it is explicitly not the paper's focus and "needs further analysis to confirm." Do not overstate transfer strength.
- The outlier-dimension mechanism is admitted to be not understood (Limitations); cite only as an empirical observation, not a mechanistic result.
- No DDI, no knowledge graph, no molecular content -- relevance is conceptual (adaptation lives in a small learned subspace), supporting the small-rank choice, not any DDI number.
